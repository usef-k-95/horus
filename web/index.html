01

Why the advice is better

Horus is a technology company, not a consultancy with AI tools. It does not sell hours. It operates an AI system that produces the advice, and the system's advantages are structural: it reads, remembers, appraises and reasons at a scale no team reaches. They show in every document it seals.

It reads the whole record

Every comparator jurisdiction, every evaluation, statute, dataset and consultation response in scope, in every language it was published in, before the first interview. A team reads what fits in the weeks it has. Horus reads everything, then begins.

It forgets nothing

Every statement from every interview and every figure from every source stays in the record and is present at the moment of judgement. Nothing is lost in a handover or flattened into a summary slide.

It appraises every option

The full option space, not the two there was time for. One method, one assumptions register, a sensitivity on every number, so that options are compared like with like.

It reasons at the frontier

Horus brings the reasoning capacity of the most capable AI systems to your engagement and spends all of it there: on your scope, your stakeholders and your numbers, for the whole of the engagement.

It argues against itself

Before anything is sealed, Horus reviews its own reasoning as a critic who has not seen the draft would, and fixes what fails. The challenges and the revisions are in the record.

It works in weeks

Reading, meeting, appraising and writing run in parallel, and nothing waits for a calendar. An engagement that would take a quarter runs in weeks, to the same standard at the end of every one.

From scheduling to synthesis

Horus writes the invitations, finds the slots, joins the calls and writes the records. The morning after the last meeting the consultation synthesis is ready: who holds which position, where they agree, where they do not.

It follows the thread

It hears the answer that was not in the plan, tests it against what others have said, and changes the next question. The pilot, the Friday-night failure and the survey were not in the plan. They are in the record.

Positions are weighed, not collected

What a stakeholder says is recorded as their position, attributed and dated, then reconciled against the other consultations and the documentary evidence before it shapes an option.

Any number of stakeholders

Twelve stakeholders or two hundred: the method is the same, the record is the same, and nothing is lost between the first meeting and the last.

04

Six kinds of engagement, one method of its own

Horus does not borrow its method from the strategy firms or the treasuries. It runs one method of its own across every engagement: scope, evidence, consultation, options, appraisal, recommendation, seal. The kind of engagement sets what is appraised and what is delivered.

Policy and regulation

A rule, a mandate, a licensing regime, a reform. Who gains, who pays, what it costs to comply, how it is phased and enforced.

Policy paper · impact appraisal · implementation plan

Strategy

A national or sector strategy: objectives, targets, the portfolio of initiatives and the trade-offs between them, appraised for coherence and feasibility.

Strategy document · options appraisal · roadmap

Institutions

Mandates, structures and operating models of ministries and agencies: who should do what, with which powers and resources.

Institutional review · operating model · transition plan

Delivery

A programme that is behind, over budget or stuck. The diagnosis, the intervention, the owners and the dates.

Delivery review · intervention plan · routines

Investment

A project or programme that needs the case for funding: value, cost, affordability, deliverability and the way to procure it.

Investment case · financial model · procurement route

Public services

A service for citizens or businesses, redesigned around the people who use it, with the cost to serve and the capability to run it.

Service design · cost to serve · capability plan

What the seal certifies

  1. Every claim traces to a source, snapshotted the day it was read.
  2. Every option in scope has been appraised by the same method, from one assumptions register.
  3. Every stakeholder named in the scope has been consulted, and their positions reconciled.
  4. The recommendation has passed Horus's adversarial review of its own reasoning.
  5. Every number reproduces from the register, and what could not be verified is written down as a gap.

Horus decides, and shows you why

A recommendation is a judgement, and Horus makes it. What makes a judgement worth trusting is how it was reached: from the whole record, by one method, tested against itself and written down so that every step can be followed and every number checked. Every Horus recommendation comes with its decision record.

Decision record · DEC-001 · DCT-2026-001week 5

Recommendation

Phase the mandate over three years, starting with firms already filing VAT online, with a free invoicing tool and a staffed support line from the first day.

Why

  1. Phasing reaches the eighty percent threshold within two years in every comparator. A single date reaches it a year sooner at twice the compliance cost, borne mostly by the smallest firms.EVD-0142 · APL-003
  2. Support, not price, is the binding constraint for small merchants. The 2024 pilot failed on support, not on the tool.MTG-003 · MTG-005 · MTG-009 · SRC-088
  3. A free tool without a support line repeats the pilot. The support line costs four percent of the programme and removes its largest risk.ASM-022 · RSK-005

Options considered

rejectedSingle-date mandateTwice the compliance cost on the smallest firms, for a one-year gain.
rejectedVoluntary scheme with incentivesFour years to the threshold in every comparator.
rejectedPhased mandate, tool onlyRepeats the failure of the 2024 pilot.
recommendedPhased mandate, tool and support lineThreshold in two years, lowest cost on the smallest firms, largest risk removed.
deferredExemption below the VAT thresholdCompliance cost unmeasured. Revisit on the first year's data.

What would change the recommendation

A support-line cost above seven percent of the programme, or pilot data showing retention above eighty-five percent without support.

Adversarial review

Fourteen challenges raised by Horus against its own draft. Three led to revisions: the phasing of medium firms, the treatment of the exemption, the cost range of the support line.

Phase the mandate over three years, starting with firms already filing online
Electronic invoicing reaches most of its benefit when adoption passes eighty percent of invoiced value. The evidence favours a phased mandate: firms already filing value-added tax online in year one, medium firms in year two, and the remaining small merchants in year three with a free tool and a staffed support line. Trust and data

A policy engagement runs on sensitive material. These are the rules Horus works under, written down so that you can hold it to them.

Where your material lives

In a dedicated engagement workspace, as readable files, with an append-only ledger of every event. Not in a shared pool across clients. Not used to train any model.

What leaves the engagement

Only what the plan says: meeting invitations to the stakeholders you named, requests for documents, and the deliverables to you. Every outbound message is in the ledger.

Confidentiality

The scope, the consultations and the deliverables are confidential to the engagement. Horus does not reuse one client's material for another.

Meetings

Recordings and transcripts stay inside the engagement and are deleted at its close unless you ask otherwise. Consultation records and the synthesis are handed over in full.

Audit

Every record carries its source and its time. Every claim in a deliverable can be followed back to the snapshot it rests on. The whole engagement can be exported and checked by anyone you choose.

Public record only

Stakeholders are profiled from the public record and from what they say in the consultation. Nothing further.
