# Benchmarks digest

What the benchmark catalogue (docs/BENCHMARKS.md) shows, condensed into the rules Horus follows for decks, documents and decision memos, each referenced to the catalogue items A1 to C4.

When Horus uses this: when the Principal frames a deliverable or defends a format choice to a client, when `producer` audits a deliverable against the standard, and when a pathway pack lists its benchmark documents.

Verification: items marked [S] in the catalogue were not opened and nothing here is quoted from them; grades A to C are the catalogue's.

## Rules for decks (A1 to A6)

1. **State the evidence base before the findings.** A1 opens with the survey, case and interview counts; A4 states survey scale on the landing page. Horus: the executive summary names interviews held, sources used and data analysed.
2. **Titles and exhibit captions are full sentences.** A1 captions exhibits as sentences; A4 cites exhibits inline. Horus: action titles and exhibit headings are sentences.
3. **Road-markers, parallel titles, sourced numbers, a "so what" per page, one framework, a quantified case for change, back-up pages.** All observed in the USPS deck (A3). Horus: the `road-marker`, implication panels, source lines, one organising framework per deck, and the `backup` archetype.
4. **Stamp every draft.** "Draft", "Working Draft" and "Preliminary Draft" appear throughout the Boston Public Schools decks (A3). Horus: three stamps tied to the version status.
5. **Frame findings as sized options for leadership.** Each BPS lever was sized in dollars per year (A3). Horus: every option carries quantified impacts; recommendations are worded as decisions.
6. **Key lines of three to five, parallel and memorable.** Five disciplines (A1), a three-by-three framework defined one sentence per element (A5), three imperatives (A6). Horus: key lines of three to five MECE statements; alliteration is optional and never bought with accuracy.
7. **The brief form.** Situation, complication, thesis, three imperatives, one figure, about 1,000 words (A6). Horus: the executive summary follows SCQA and stays under 1,000 words.
8. **Issue the executive summary as its own file.** A1 and A2 publish separate summary and full-report PDFs. Horus: the executive summary is always a separate deliverable.
9. **Follow the observed engagement rhythm.** RFP, proposal, one-slide workplan, weekly check-in decks of 9 to 11 slides, a steering working draft, a 30-slide draft and final, a 10 to 13 page summary (A3, BPS). Horus: kickoff deck, weekly status note, phase readouts as working drafts, final deck, executive summary.

## Rules for policy and appraisal documents (B1 to B13)

10. **A stand-alone summary sized for the decider.** Four to five pages for the EU (B3); under 1,000 words opening with a 25-word problem statement at HKS (B11). Horus: under 1,000 words, first sentence the problem.
11. **Problem, baseline and rationale before anything else.** OECD steps 2 and 3 (B1); EU section 2 with "evaluate first" (B3); A-4 baseline and need (B7); the Green Book strategic case (B6); Bardach step 1 (B12). Horus: no option is discussed before the baseline is accepted at AP2.
12. **Objectives are SMART.** UK template section 3 (B5), Green Book (B6), EU section 4 (B3). Horus: `brief.objectives` carry indicators.
13. **Options run long list to shortlist to preferred, always against do-nothing.** Green Book options framework (B6); UK template sections 5 and 6 (B5); EU sections 5 to 7 (B3); "never ignore do nothing" in Working with Ministers (C1). Horus: an option of kind `do_nothing` in every comparison.
14. **Impacts are tabulated by who bears them.** A-4 accounting statement (B7); UK scorecard with business and household measures (B5); EU annex "who is affected and how" (B3). Horus: the `distributional-incidence` archetype and the who-is-affected annex.
15. **Uncertainty is handled formally.** Sensitivity and switching values, optimism bias and a discount-rate schedule in the Green Book (B6); a default real rate in the 2023 A-4 text (B7). Horus: `sensitivity-tornado`; rates held as assumptions; A-4 2023 cited as method only, with the Green Book 2026 checked for rate changes.
16. **Proportionate RIA with enforcement built in.** RIA need not always be a full quantitative CBA, and every RIA has an enforcement and compliance strategy (B1); SME test and competition check (B3, B4). Horus: `appraisals.proportionality_level` and the `regulatory` block.
17. **Report stakeholder views by group.** Consultation synopsis as an annex, views by group (B3). Horus: positions by stakeholder category; individuals only under attribution rules.
18. **Design monitoring and evaluation at the start.** OECD step 8 (B1); EU section 9 (B3); UK template section 8 (B5); Green Book chapter 8 (B6); CPI "measurement" (A5). Horus: `appraisals.monitoring` filled before AP4 closes.
19. **Presentation ceilings and conventions.** Main report at most 40 pages excluding annexes, non-technical language, titled and sourced tables and figures, glossary and abbreviations (B3); Arial 12 and continuously numbered paragraphs (B5). Horus: the document rules in design-system.md.
20. **Report paradigm and the outcomes matrix.** Introduction, parallel analytical chapters, conclusions, recommendations, implementation and alternatives, appendices (B11); the Eightfold Path with its outcomes matrix (B12). Horus: the policy-note structure; `appraisal-comparison` is the outcomes matrix.
21. **Institutional conventions.** Executive summary, sectioned analysis, boxes and annexes with an authorship disclaimer (B9); recommendations tabled by short and medium term (B8). Horus: recommendation tables split short term and medium term.

## Rules for decision memos (C1 to C4)

22. **The ministerial submission form.** Title; issue in at most 100 words stating the financial impact; recommendation as "agree that" or "note that" including do-nothing; timing as a date, not "urgent"; discussion of at most two A4 pages readable in under five minutes covering impact, financial implications, evidence, risks, communications and handling, parliamentary and legal points; annexes (C1, DWP template and Working with Ministers). Horus: the decision-memo structure.
23. **Recommendations are action-oriented, readable in isolation and not used to argue; "noting" is minimised.** Submissions also cover benefits, disadvantages and risks, financial impacts and offsets, implementation with responsibilities, legislative implications and wider impacts (C2, paragraph 97). Horus: implementation and legal sections are mandatory in the memo.
24. **Separate the recommendation from the analysis.** The analysis carries no opinion; the communications plan is an annex (C3). Horus: the discussion is analytic; opinion lives only in the recommendation.
25. **Two pages for the head of government.** Papers to the President held to two pages with attachments for the rest (C4, Ford Staff Secretary); decision memoranda of one to three pages (C4, NSDMs). Horus: two pages, hard ceiling.
26. **Recommendation wording is the draft decision.** New South Wales requires it; Queensland fixes twelve headings, an eight-page body and a one-page cover sheet (C2). Horus: decisions-sought text is written so it can be minuted unchanged.
27. **The secretariat may refuse a paper on form.** Cabinet Manual paragraphs 4.28 to 4.31 (C1). Horus: the logic audit plays the secretariat.

## Caveats and gaps

- A2, parts of A3 and A5, the World Bank RIA case study (B8) and the Canadian template (C3) are [S] or unverified; they are not quoted.
- OMB Circular A-4 (2023) appears rescinded; Horus cites it as analytical method, not as current law (B7).
- The Green Book 2026 supersedes the 2022 edition; the discount-rate schedule must be re-checked before use (B6).
- The catalogue is strongest on the regulatory pathway and executive memos. Strategy, operating-model, delivery, business-case and service-design benchmarks are to be added before those packs are finalised.
- The USPS and Boston Public Schools decks are known through teardowns and inventories; the originals should be read before the archetype library is declared final.
