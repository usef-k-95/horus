# McKinsey's five disciplines of government transformation

From the McKinsey Center for Government's "Delivering for citizens" (2018): committed leadership, clear purpose and priorities, cadence and coordination in delivery, compelling communication, and capability for change. Horus uses the five disciplines as the structure for assessing transformation readiness and for the management and capability sections of delivery plans.

When Horus uses this: P2 readiness diagnosis and P5 implementation planning on the transformation and delivery pathway; the strategy pathway's "resources and governance" chapter.

## What it is

The report draws on a survey of about 3,000 public officials in 18 countries, 80 case studies and 30 leader interviews, and reports that about 80 per cent of government transformations fail to meet their objectives (headline figures as stated in the source). It attributes success to five disciplines. Committed leadership: visible, persistent sponsorship from the top, with leaders who spend time on the transformation and model the change. Clear purpose and priorities: a small number of outcomes, translated into targets that the organisation understands. Cadence and coordination in delivery: routines, a delivery engine, cross-agency coordination and early problem-solving. Compelling communication: a narrative for staff and citizens that explains why, what and how, repeated through credible messengers. Capability for change: skills, talent management, incentives and culture that allow the organisation to sustain the change. The disciplines are a synthesis of practice rather than a method; their value to Horus is as a diagnostic grid and as the client-facing vocabulary for the management case.

## When Horus uses it

- Transformation and delivery pathway: the readiness assessment at P2 rates the client against the five disciplines with evidence and names the weakest as a binding constraint; the delivery plan's "roles and capability" and the capability plan deliverable address the gaps.
- Strategy pathway: the implementation roadmap's governance and capability sections use the disciplines as headings.
- Public investment pathway: the management case's delivery arrangements.
- Institutional pathway: transition plans, where leadership commitment and capability decide success.
- Pre-recommendation check on any pathway alongside the CPI Fundamentals: the two frameworks overlap on legitimacy and action and differ in emphasis.

## Steps

1. **Define the transformation** in scope: the outcomes, the organisations involved and the time frame.
2. **Assess committed leadership**: who sponsors, how much time they give, what they have said and done; succession risk.
3. **Assess purpose and priorities**: whether the targets are few, measurable, understood at the front line.
4. **Assess cadence and coordination**: existing routines, data flows, cross-agency mechanisms, problem-solving practice.
5. **Assess communication**: whether there is a narrative, who carries it, how staff and the public hear it.
6. **Assess capability**: skills and vacancies in critical roles, incentives, culture, change management experience.
7. **Rate each discipline** on a simple scale with evidence; identify the binding constraint and its implications for sequencing.
8. **Design responses** per gap in the implementation plan and the capability plan.

## Outputs → state records

- `analyses` of kind `diagnostic` (readiness assessment) with `content` organised by the five disciplines and `findings[]` with evidence and confidence.
- `hypotheses` of kind `implementation` for the suspected binding constraint.
- `analyses` of kind `implementation_plan` with `institutional_arrangements` (leadership and cadence), `capacity_needs[]` (capability), `kpis[]` (priorities); `analyses` of kind `communication_plan` (compelling communication).
- `appraisals.feasibility.administrative` and `appraisals.delivery.effort` on the transformation pathway.
- `deliverables` of type `delivery_plan` (roles and capability section), `implementation_roadmap` on the strategy pathway, and the capability plan within the delivery plan set.
- `risks` of scope `implementation` for leadership turnover and capability gaps.

## Pitfalls

- Using the headline failure statistic as a scare line without the attribution and the survey basis.
- Treating the disciplines as a checklist of documents (a communications plan exists) rather than of behaviours (leaders spend time, messengers are credible).
- Rating leadership commitment from the sponsor's own statements; triangulate with time spent and decisions taken.
- Confusing capability with headcount; incentives and culture decide more.
- Presenting the framework as proprietary magic; it is a synthesis of common findings, and the client will have seen it before.
