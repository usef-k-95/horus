## Part B — RIA and policy-document standards

### B1. OECD (2020), Regulatory Impact Assessment: Best Practice Principles for Regulatory Policy — grade A [F, 36 pages]
https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2020/02/regulatory-impact-assessment_0bf78a03/7a9638cb-en.pdf (landing pages returned 403)

Report: foreword; acknowledgements; executive summary; background and context; best-practice principles in five sections (commitment and buy-in; governance and system design; embedding RIA through capacity and accountability; targeted and appropriate methodology; continuous monitoring, evaluation and improvement).

Box 2.2, basic steps: 1 consultations and stakeholder engagement; 2 problem definition; 3 objective; 4 description of the regulatory proposal; 5 identification of alternatives; 6 analysis of benefits and costs; 7 identification of the preferred solution; 8 monitoring and evaluation framework. Conventions: proportionality; RIA "should not always be interpreted as requiring a full-fledged, quantitative cost-benefit analysis"; an enforcement and compliance strategy in every RIA; iterative; results well communicated.

### B2. OECD 2012 Recommendation on Regulatory Policy and Governance (OECD/LEGAL/0390) — grade A [F]
https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/273/273.en.pdf

Twelve principles: whole-of-government regulatory policy at the highest political level; open government and consultation; oversight institutions; RIA integrated at early stages (goals, necessity, alternatives, trade-offs); ex post stock reviews; performance reporting; regulatory agencies; review of legality and procedural fairness; risk-based design; multi-level coherence; sub-national capacity; international standards. An annex elaborates each.

### B3. European Commission, Better Regulation Guidelines SWD(2021) 305 and Toolbox — grade A [F]
Guidelines https://commission.europa.eu/system/files/2021-11/swd2021_305_en.pdf; Toolbox 2023 chapter 2 https://commission.europa.eu/system/files/2023-09/BRT-2023-Chapter%202-How%20to%20carry%20out%20an%20impact%20assessment.pdf; toolbox index https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-making-process/better-regulation/better-regulation-guidelines-and-toolbox/better-regulation-toolbox_en (now shows the December 2025 edition with 69 tools).

Impact-assessment report (Tool 11): 1 introduction, political and legal context; 2 what is the problem and why is it a problem (problem tree; evaluate first); 3 why should the EU act (subsidiarity grid); 4 what should be achieved; 5 what are the options; 6 what are the impacts of the options and who will be affected; 7 how do the options compare; 8 the preferred option (including proportionality); 9 how would impacts be monitored and evaluated. Mandatory annexes: procedural information; stakeholder consultation synopsis report; who is affected and how (summary cost and benefit table); analytical methods; competitiveness check.

Conventions: main report benchmark 40 pages excluding annexes; executive summary of four to five pages as a separate document translated into all EU languages; non-technical language; table of contents, abbreviations, glossary; tables and figures titled, sourced and self-explanatory; stakeholder views reported by group; coverage of economic, social and environmental impacts, SMEs and competitiveness, fundamental rights, climate and digital checks; Regulatory Scrutiny Board review. Analytical tools include the SME test, competition, income distribution, monitoring indicators, cost typology, Standard Cost Model, baselines, cost-benefit analysis, discount factors and uncertainty.

### B4. UK Better Regulation Framework guidance (2023 reform) — grade A [F, 64 pages]
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67587ba55a2e4d4b993bfa83/better-regulation-framework-guidance-2023.pdf (collection https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/better-regulation-framework)

Chapters: introduction; scope; summary; key information; policy development; whether RPC options-assessment scrutiny is required; options assessment; RPC independent scrutiny; collective agreement; development of the regulatory impact assessment; finalising the policy; introduction to Parliament; post-implementation reviews; annexes (exclusions; terms; small and micro business assessment; technical note; trade; templates and calculator; other guidance). Stages: initial policy development → pre-implementation (options assessment for RPC scrutiny before collective agreement and consultation; final IA laid in Parliament) → post-implementation review. Thresholds: plus or minus £10m equivalent annual net direct cost to business as de minimis (self-certify below); plus or minus £100m with a weak options-assessment rating or an exclusion triggers final-stage RPC scrutiny. The IA documents net present social value, business NPV, equivalent annual net direct cost to business and to households, plus proportionate distributional and net-zero analysis; built on ROAMEF and the Green Book.

### B6. HM Treasury Green Book — grade A [F]
2022 edition (archived, 148 pages) https://webarchive.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ukgwa/20241231215819mp_/https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6645c709bd01f5ed32793cbc/Green_Book_2022__updated_links_.pdf; superseded by the **Green Book 2026** (February 2026, 88 pages, ten chapters and Annex A) https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-green-book--2/the-green-book-2026. ## Synthesis — common structural elements

1. Every genre opens with a stand-alone summary sized for the decider: EU four to five pages, HKS under 1,000 words, UK submission issue at most 100 words, Cabinet cover sheets at most one page.
2. Problem and rationale precede everything else: problem definition, baseline or business as usual and "why intervene" are the mandatory first analytic steps (OECD, EU section 2, A-4 sections 4 and 5, Green Book strategic case, Bardach step 1).
3. Objectives are stated explicitly and made measurable (SMART in the Green Book and UK templates; EU section 4; Bardach criteria).
4. Options are compared against a do-nothing counterfactual, moving long list → shortlist → preferred option (Green Book, UK IA sections 5 and 6, EU sections 5 to 7, "never ignore do nothing" in Working with Ministers).
5. Impacts are tabulated by who bears them (costs and benefits, SMEs, households, distribution, competitiveness) with monetised NPV and BCR plus explicit non-monetised effects (A-4 accounting statement, UK scorecard, EU annex 3).
6. Uncertainty is handled formally: sensitivity and switching values, optimism bias, discount-rate schedules (3.5 falling to 2.5 per cent in the UK; 2 per cent in the US 2023 text).
7. Decision documents end with action-worded recommendations the principal can accept or reject (agree or note; approve or disapprove; "it is recommended that"), kept separate from the argument.
8. Implementation, timing, consultation, communications and handling, and legal and financial clearances are standing sections, not afterthoughts (Australia paragraph 97; Canada annexes; DWP checklist).
9. Monitoring and evaluation plans are required at the design stage (OECD step 8; EU section 9; UK IA section 8; Green Book chapter 8; CPI "measurement").
10. Presentation conventions converge: hard length ceilings (two to ten page memos; 40-page IA reports), numbered paragraphs, titled and sourced exhibits and tables, consistent typography (Arial 11 to 12 or Times 12), tracker or section markers and narrative action titles in consulting decks, and draft and version stamps throughout.
