The idea

Horus is a technology company. Its product is an AI system that runs policy engagements for governments end to end: it takes a scope of work, plans the engagement, reads the whole record, conducts the stakeholder consultations, appraises the options and delivers the sealed policy document. The brand has one job: make that credible at first sight.

Position

A technology company replacing parts of consulting for governments, built AI-native. Not a consultancy with AI tools, not a chatbot. A company whose work carries a seal because every claim in it can be checked. The company is called Horus, never Horus Advisory.

Promise

Advice that can be checked: every claim traced to its source, every option appraised by one method, every stakeholder heard, delivered in weeks by one system that runs the engagement end to end.

Tension to hold

Ultra-modern and ancient at once. The technology is the newest there is; the role, counsel to the state, is the oldest profession in government. The brand keeps both in frame.

02

The name and its meaning

Horus is the falcon god of the Egyptian sky: the one who sees from above. His name, Ḥr, is usually read as "the distant one", the bird so high it takes in the whole land at once. That is the company's idea of oversight: the full picture, held calmly, from a height that lets you see how the parts fit.

The pharaoh ruled as the living Horus. For the first dynasties the king's most important name, the Horus name, was written inside a serekh: a rectangular frame drawn as the niched facade of the palace, with the falcon perched on top. It is the oldest royal seal in the world and the direct ancestor of every government stamp since. The Horus seal borrows its niche pattern.

The Eye of Horus, the wedjat, was the eye torn out in his fight with Set and made whole again by Thoth. Egyptians wore it for protection and wholeness, and used its parts as fractions: one half, one quarter, one eighth, down to one sixty-fourth. The parts add up to sixty-three sixty-fourths. The missing fraction, the story goes, was restored by magic. Horus takes that as a rule about evidence: say what the record shows, and say where the last sixty-fourth is missing.

Oversight

The falcon's view. The company sees across ministries, stakeholders and sources, and reports from above the fray.

Authority

The serekh and the royal name. Work that is sealed and stood behind.

Wholeness and honesty

The restored eye and its fractions. Complete where it can be, explicit about the gap where it cannot.

What we do not borrow

Pyramids, hieroglyph fonts, sand, papyrus textures, pharaoh imagery. The brand is Egyptian in idea, not in costume.

Clear space and minimum size

Keep a clear zone of one lens diameter (2r) around the mark on every side. Nothing enters it: no text, no rule, no edge of a photograph. The full mark is never set below 32 pixels or 9 millimetres; below that, use the solid mark, and below 16 pixels the small mark.

Placement

The mark sits at a corner, never centred on a page unless it is the only thing on it. On slides it signs the bottom-left, the way a letterhead signs the top-left. On documents the seal, not the mark, closes the page.

The seal

The seal is the mark in office. It goes only on work that has passed every Horus check: a policy document, a decision brief, a final deck, a letter of advice. It is the company's equivalent of the eagle on a government stamp, and it is used with the same restraint.

The Horus seal · outer ring, the niche ring of the serekh, the solid eye at the centre
On documents · ink or deep gold on platinum paper

Anatomy

Outer ring
2.4 units at 100; the boundary of what Horus vouches for
Niche ring
36 bars, the palace facade of the serekh, the oldest royal seal
Centre
The solid mark; never the outline mark, which breaks up when stamped
Text ring
Optional, outside the niche ring: HORUS, and the engagement code at the foot. No descriptor
Focus point
Always the seal colour. The lapis live state never appears on a seal

Rules of use

The seal is applied once per document, at the close of the page or the foot of the cover, never in a header, never repeated. Minimum size is 28 millimetres in print and 96 pixels on screen; the preferred print size is 36 millimetres. In print it may be blind-embossed or foiled in gold; on screen it is flat colour.

What carries the seal: final policy documents, decision briefs, final decks, letters of advice, once every check has passed. What does not: drafts, working papers, emails, slides inside a deck, the website, social media. A sealed document carries the attestation line beside the seal, in Plex Mono: the checks passed, the engagement code, the date and the version.

Wordmark and lockups

The wordmark is the name set in Saira at its widest width, capitals, letterspaced. The expanded width gives it the engineered, aerospace feel; the spacing gives it the calm of an inscription.

HORUS
Gold wordmark · only when the mark is absent, and only on obsidian

The wordmark never appears in lower case, never in a condensed width and never in any other typeface. Minimum size is 14 pixels or 4 millimetres cap height. In running text the company is written Horus, in normal capitalisation, like any name. It is a technology company, and the words around the name say so: never the company, the practice or the consultancy.

Proportion

Roughly ninety parts obsidian and basalt, seven parts platinum and silver, three parts gold, and lapis as a point, never an area. Gold is a line, a mark, a single figure, a rule under a title. It is never a background for text and never a paragraph colour. Where an audience expects warmth, the warmth comes from pale gold at small sizes, not from more gold.

Semantic colour

Good #3DA66B, warning #D08A2A and critical #C8453A exist for state in interfaces and charts. They are not accent colours and never decorate.

Typography

Three faces, each with one job. Saira, an engineered sans with a width axis, for display and interface. Source Serif 4 for anything read at length: the company's advice is read by ministers, and serious reading deserves a serif. IBM Plex Mono for data, codes, dates and attestation.

Saira

Display, headings, wordmark, interface controls, table text. Weights 400 to 600; width 100 for text, 112 to 125 for titles and the wordmark, 85 for dense tables. Never below 85.

Rules

Headings in sentence case, and they state the finding, not the topic. Capitals are reserved for the wordmark, the display register and data keys. Numbers are set tabular wherever they align. Emphasis is weight, never colour and never underlining. No more than two sizes on a slide; no more than four on a page.

Never

Inter, Space Grotesk, Instrument Serif and the other default faces of generated design; condensed capitals for drama; letterspaced lower case; gold text in paragraphs; random bold words in a sentence; small capitals used as decoration.

08

Graphic system

Four devices carry the brand beyond the mark. Each comes from the same sources and each does work; none is decoration.

The rule

A one-pixel line in graphite, or in gold under a title. It separates, it measures, it signs. Tables are built from rules alone, with no fills and no zebra striping.

The niche

The serekh's facade as a pattern: vertical bars at a fixed pitch. Used as a divider, a header band on a cover, or the ring of the seal. Never as a full-page texture.

The fractions

The parts of the eye as a scale of confidence: 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, 1/64. Used in charts and evidence displays to show how much of a claim rests on which grade of source, and to make the missing sixty-fourth visible.

Charts

Grey bars on obsidian, one bar in gold where the answer is. Axes are rules, labels are Plex Mono, there is no grid behind the data and no 3D. The same restraint the best roadshow decks use: the number does the work.

Imagery

Photography, when used at all, is of institutions and territory: ministry architecture, council chambers, ports, grids, satellite and night-light views of the countries the company works in. Monochrome or desaturated, with generous darkness; the mark and type sit in the dark part of the frame.

Never: handshakes, stock boardrooms, robots, glowing brains, circuit boards, pyramids. Illustration is line drawing in a single weight, gold on obsidian, drawn the way the mark is drawn.

Motion

Motion is rare, slow and literal. It shows a state or reveals a structure; it never performs. Ninety percent of Horus surfaces do not move at all.

On the website, the focus point of the mark follows the pointer within a small radius. It is the one signature motion, and it means oversight

10

Voice

Horus writes the way a trusted adviser speaks to a minister: the answer first, the reasoning second, the evidence always within reach. Plain words, full sentences, numbers with units, and no attempt to impress.

Principles

Answer first. The first sentence of anything is the conclusion. The structure beneath it is the pyramid every good consultant learns.

Specific. "Firms already filing VAT online adopt within a quarter" beats "adoption is faster for digitised firms".

Sourced. A claim without a source is an opinion, and Horus labels it as one.

Argued. A recommendation comes with the reasoning that produced it and the evidence it rests on, so that any reader can follow the steps and check the numbers.

Calm. No exclamation marks, no urgency, no superlatives, no hedging into mush. Where the evidence is thin, it says so in one sentence.

Standard lines

Opening a consultation

"Good morning, and thank you for the time. I am Horus. The Ministry has asked me to design the electronic invoicing mandate for small merchants, and your members will carry most of its compliance cost, so I want to understand adoption from their side. What is the single biggest obstacle today?"

Opening a brief

"Recommendation: phase the e-invoicing mandate over three years, starting with firms already filing VAT online. This brief sets out the evidence, the options considered and the risks of each."

When evidence is thin

"No primary source holds the compliance cost for micro-enterprises. The estimate below is bounded between the two nearest comparators, flagged in the assumptions register, and should be read as indicative."

Slides

Obsidian ground. The title is the finding, in Saira 500 at width 106, top-left, two lines at most. One chart or one exhibit per slide, grey with a single gold answer. The source line and the page number sit on the bottom rule in Plex Mono. The mark signs the bottom-left of every slide; the seal appears only on the cover of a sealed deck, once every check has passed.

Tier 0 decks for a head of government carry no more than twelve slides and no slide carries more than forty words.

Policy notes and briefs

Platinum paper, ink text in Source Serif 4, a Saira title that states the recommendation. Two columns above A4 width, one column below. Data keys in Plex Mono. The seal closes the first page of a final note with the attestation line beside it; drafts carry the word DRAFT in the header and no seal.

In Google Meet and Zoom the company appears as Horus, with the serekh plaque as its avatar on obsidian. The name is never decorated with a descriptor or a role.
